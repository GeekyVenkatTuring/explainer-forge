## Description
How do you measure information — not what a message *means*, but how *much* it carries,
as a number? In 1948 Claude Shannon answered that question and invented the digital age.

This is a complete, from-scratch course in information theory, built one clear idea at a
time — from a single surprising coin flip all the way to the limits of communication,
the loss functions behind modern AI, and the physics of a single bit. No hand-waving:
every algorithm is actually computed on screen (Huffman trees, Hamming codes, arithmetic
coding, entropy and capacity curves).
