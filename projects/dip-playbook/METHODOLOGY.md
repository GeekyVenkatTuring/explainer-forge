# The Macro-Dip Playbook — methodology (internal)

Companion to `macro-dip-playbook.mp4`. Use this to actually run the screen the video
teaches. **This is education, not investment advice. Verify every figure yourself.**

> Why there is no stock list here: for the week in question, per-stock market data
> (exact weekly %, PE, quarterly, peer-discount) could **not be verified** to an
> acceptable accuracy standard from available tools — the numbers returned were
> internally contradictory and matched placeholder examples. Rather than publish a

> generate and vet the list from a source you trust.

## Last week's macro backdrop (qualitative / indicative)
A ~2–3% index fall over five sessions — worst week in months — driven by **external
macro forces, not company results**:
1. **Crude near $100** → pressures oil-marketing, paints, tyres, aviation, logistics; helps upstream (ONGC/Oil India).
2. **Weak rupee (~96/$)** → hurts importers & dollar-debt names; helps exporters (IT, pharma). *Note the puzzle: rupee helps IT, yet IT fell — see #5.*
3. **FII net selling** → indiscriminate; hits index heavyweights, financials, large-cap leaders (great businesses included). DIIs absorb.
4. **War / geopolitics** → risk-off, volatility spike; fear is priced fast and forgiven slowly.
5. **US curbs on IT** → the caution flag: is it a temporary DIP or a structural RE-RATING? A cheap price doesn't help if earnings power is permanently lower.

## The one distinction: macro DIP vs company DAMAGE
| Likely a DIP (candidate) | Likely DAMAGE (avoid) |
|---|---|
| Whole sector/market fell together | Only THIS stock fell hard |
| No company-specific bad news | Earnings miss / guidance cut |
| Earnings & guidance intact | Governance / accounting red flags |
| External, temporary trigger | Permanent shift in the business |
| Balance sheet unchanged | Rising debt / cash-flow stress |

**Fast test:** did the peers fall too? Whole sector down → macro. Only this name cratered → dig deeper.

## Filter 1 — is it a strong business?
- ROE > ~15%  · Debt-to-Equity < ~0.5  · consistent 3–5yr profit growth  · positive free cash flow  · durable moat/leader.

## Filter 2 — is it actually cheap (not just "fallen")?
- PE < industry/peer median  · reasonable PEG  · P/B < its own 5yr avg  · well below 52-wk high.
- "It fell 5%" ≠ cheap. "A leader below its peers and its own history" = cheap.

## The screen (Screener.in custom query)
```
Market Capitalization > 20000 AND
Return on equity > 15 AND
Debt to equity < 0.5 AND
Profit growth > 10 AND
Price to Earning < Industry PE
```
Then **intersect with last week's top losers** (Trendlyne → Top Losers → Week → Nifty 200).
Keep only names in **both** lists: strong, cheap, freshly dipped. The screen produces a
**shortlist, never a buy** — verify each name by hand.

## Before buying
1. Confirm **why** it fell (macro/sector = ok; company-specific = stop).
2. Wait for price to **stabilise** — don't catch a falling knife.
3. Enter in **tranches**, not all at once.
4. Write the **thesis + exit** (what would prove you wrong).

## Risk
Size each position; spread across the shortlist; give the thesis time; exit on thesis
break, not a one-day wobble.
