# Skill 13 — International IPO Research & Design (US + China) — read BEFORE any US/China IPO video

This is the **international** counterpart to skills/12 (which is India-only: rupees,
NSE/BSE, Chittorgarh, SEBI, GMP). Do NOT use the India sources, currencies, or the
`sm` scene set for a US/China video. Use this skill and the `gi` scene set instead.

A wrong number in a finance video misleads real investors — it is the worst defect
this repo can ship. The market-data web tools in this harness have returned
fabricated-looking figures before (see the `market-data-tool-unreliable` memory), so
for US/China IPOs the default posture is **concept/education framing**: anchor on real,
named, well-reported IPOs and the *frameworks* for evaluating them; keep precise
figures qualitative or clearly labeled ("~", "reportedly", "as scheduled"), and never
present a listing-gain prediction. Say the disclaimer on screen and in narration.

## 1. The two markets differ structurally — that difference IS the teaching hook

| Axis | United States | China (Mainland + Hong Kong) |
|---|---|---|
| Where they list | Nasdaq, NYSE | STAR Market (Shanghai, SSE 688), ChiNext (Shenzhen), Beijing SE; **Hong Kong (HKEX)** for global/dual listings |
| Regulator / process | SEC — file an **S-1**, roadshow, **book-building** sets a price *range*, underwriters allocate | **CSRC** registration-based (post-2023 reform); mainland pricing is guidance-bounded; strategic placement common |
| Typical week's slate | **Many small/mid deals**, biotech-heavy, plus **SPACs** (blank-check) | **A few very large, strategic deals** — semiconductors, EV, hard-tech, often policy-aligned |
| First-day trading | No daily price limit | **STAR/ChiNext: no daily limit for first 5 days**, then ±20% band; main boards ±10%. HK: no limit |
| Who can buy | Any brokerage; retail buys at open (not the IPO price) | Mainland A-shares gated (QFII/Stock Connect for foreigners); HK open to global; **ADRs** for US-listed Chinese names |
| Ownership quirks | **Dual-class shares** (founder super-voting) common | Many US-listed Chinese firms use a **VIE** (contract-based control, not equity) — a real risk to name |
| What drives it | Market appetite, rate cycle, sector momentum | **National priorities** (chip self-sufficiency), plus market appetite |

Currencies: US **USD ($)**; mainland China **CNY / RMB (¥, "yuan")**; Hong Kong **HKD
(HK$)**. Never mix them; name the currency every time a figure appears. STAR Market =
"Sci-Tech innovAtion boaRd" (Shanghai, ticker 688xxx) — China's Nasdaq-style board for
hard-tech; say "STAR Market" on screen, gloss it once in narration.

## 2. Vocabulary the video must define (name → plain line → concrete example → [pause])
- **IPO** — a private company sells shares to the public for the first time; you can
  now buy a slice on an exchange.
- **S-1 / prospectus** — the disclosure document filed with the SEC (China: the
  registration/prospectus filed with the exchange + CSRC).
- **Book-building & price range** — underwriters take institutional orders across a
  range (e.g. $15–$17) and set the final IPO price; the *range* is what you see pre-deal.
- **SPAC** (blank-check) — a shell company that IPOs with **no business**, raising cash
  to *later* merge with a real one; you're trusting the sponsor to find a target.
- **Lock-up** — insiders can't sell for ~90–180 days after listing; expiry can pressure
  the price.
- **STAR Market / ChiNext** — China's registration-based hard-tech boards with looser
  first-days trading rules than the main boards.
- **VIE** — "variable interest entity": many US-listed Chinese firms are controlled by
  contract, not share ownership — a structural risk to flag.
- **ADR** — American Depositary Receipt: how a Chinese/foreign company trades on a US
  exchange without a direct US listing.
Terminology budget still applies: ≤ 2 new terms per minute (skills/02).

## 3. Source directory (international)
**US IPOs** — StockAnalysis.com IPO calendar (names, ticker, exchange, price range,
shares, date — the most structured), Nasdaq.com IPO calendar, Renaissance Capital
(renaissancecapital.com), MarketBeat IPO calendar, SEC EDGAR (the S-1 itself — the
authority), Seeking Alpha "US IPO Weekly Recap" for the week's tone.
**China / HK IPOs** — HKEXnews (hkexnews.hk) for HK prospectuses, SSE/STAR
(english.sse.com.cn) and SZSE for mainland, **Caixin Global** and Reuters/Bloomberg for
mega-deal reporting, KPMG/Deloitte/EY quarterly China-HK IPO reviews for market context.
**FX** — name the currency; if converting, use a round, clearly-labeled rate ("≈", as of
the video date), never a false-precision cross-rate.

## 4. Data-honesty rules (violations = defects) — same spirit as skills/12
1. **Name the currency and exchange every time.** "$200M on Nasdaq" / "¥57.9B on the
   STAR Market" / "HK$55B in Hong Kong" — never a bare number.
2. **IPO dates are estimates.** Calendars say "estimated and may change / postpone /
   withdraw." Say "as scheduled / expected to price this week", not "will list on X".
3. **Price RANGE, not price.** Pre-deal you have a range and a deal size — present those;
   the final price prints on pricing night. Don't state a final price before it's set.
4. **Deal size ≈ shares × mid-price.** If you show a deal size, make it consistent with
   the range you showed; don't invent a third number.
5. **No listing-gain prediction.** There's no legal GMP-style number to quote; do not
   imply first-day pop. US retail buys at the *open*, usually above the IPO price — say so.
6. **VIE / dual-class / lock-up are risks to state, not decorate.** If a Chinese name
   uses a VIE, or a US name has founder super-voting, name it plainly.
7. **Triangulate mega-deals.** For a headline figure (e.g. "largest STAR Market IPO
   ever"), confirm across 2+ reputable outlets (Caixin + Reuters/Bloomberg/KPMG).
8. **Disclaimer mandatory** — education only, not investment advice, not a buy/sell call,
   not a prediction; consult a licensed advisor. In narration (title or recap) AND the
   description. Avoid "guaranteed", "sure-shot", price targets-as-advice.

## 5. The combined-video structure that works (US + China in one)
Contrast is the spine. Build it as a two-part diptych with a payoff compare:
1. Title (≤ ~20s) → 2. Hook: "same word, two different worlds this week" (a developing
split screen: US fills with many small tickets, China with one giant) → 3. IPO in one
breath (what an IPO is, computed pipeline) → **PART 1 — The American Way**: this week's
US slate (biotech names + a SPAC) · how a US IPO works (S-1 → SEC → book-build → open) ·
what a SPAC is · what the slate says (many small, market-driven) → **PART 2 — The Chinese
Way**: this week's China slate (the mega chip listings) · where China lists (STAR vs
ChiNext vs HKEX) · how it differs (CSRC, first-5-days no limit, strategic placement) ·
the strategic theme (chip self-sufficiency) → **PART 3 — Same Word, Different Game**: the
side-by-side compare (size · count · sector · who invests · what drives it) · what it
means for a global investor (ADRs, access, VIE/lock-up risks, where to watch) → Recap +
disclaimer. Keep every beat ≤ ~75s; front-load reveals to the spoken position (skills/02).

## 6. Pre-render validation checklist (run every time)
- [ ] Every figure carries its **currency + exchange**; USD/CNY/HKD never mixed
- [ ] Dates framed as "as scheduled / expected"; no "will list on <date>" certainty
- [ ] Price ranges (not final prices); deal size consistent with shares × mid
- [ ] No listing-gain/first-day-pop prediction anywhere
- [ ] Mega-deal headline figures triangulated across 2+ reputable outlets
- [ ] VIE / dual-class / lock-up risks stated where they apply
- [ ] "What the company does" is verifiable/stable (safe to state); precise financials
      kept qualitative or clearly labeled under concept/education framing
- [ ] Disclaimer in narration AND description; no buy/sell calls or price targets
